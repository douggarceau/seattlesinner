# Love, Lust & Bad Decisions
## I Thought I'd Have a Life by Now
By Zoe Rosen — The Guitar Doug Report

I moved to Seattle from a small town in Oklahoma because I wanted a bigger life. I thought that meant a better job, interesting people, a place of my own, and eventually somebody to share it with. I didn't think I'd be spending so much time calculating whether I could afford to go out.

I live in Ballard. My apartment is small, but it's mine for as long as I can keep paying the rent. I work full-time and pick up shifts at a second job. The student loans don't seem to get smaller. Sometimes I check the balance after making a payment, hoping to see something that feels like progress. Usually I close the app and go to work.

Most mornings I get a triple-shot iced brown sugar oatmilk shaken espresso at Starbucks. I order the same thing every time. I like having one decision already made. I sometimes take a book to the Ballard Library, where I can sit quietly without spending money. I wish getting there always felt as simple as it should. When the sidewalk is obstructed, I have to change my route. I feel irritated, then guilty for feeling irritated, then tired of having an argument with myself before I've even opened my book.

Seattle still frightens me sometimes. Back home, I knew who lived down the road. Here I can walk past hundreds of people and recognize no one. On the bus, I notice when someone looks at me too long. At work, I've learned how to end conversations that don't feel like conversations anymore. Some mornings I wear a big hoodie because I don't want my body to be the first thing anyone notices. I know I look good when I dress up. I just don't always want the attention.

I still go on dates. Most of them happen around Ballard Avenue, because it's close to home and I don't have the energy to cross the city after a full day of work. One evening I met a man at the Old Pequliar on Market Street. He wasn't awful. We talked about work and rent, and then he talked about his last relationship for a long time. I listened. I couldn't tell whether he wanted to know me or just wanted somebody to listen.

He texted afterward and asked whether I'd had a good time. I told him yes. I didn't know what else to say.

I used to picture myself married by my late twenties. I imagined a kitchen table, someone making coffee while I got ready for work, maybe children someday. Now I think about what childcare costs, what another person might owe, and whether two exhausted people can make a life feel less exhausting.

I don't want to give up on any of it. But sometimes I get home from a date, take off the clothes I put on because I wanted to feel pretty, and look at the next day's schedule. There's always another shift. There's always another bill. I lie awake trying to remember when I last felt excited about the future.

I still want someone to love. I just wish I knew where I was supposed to find the time.
